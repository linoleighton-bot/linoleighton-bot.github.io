How it works

Three things, and nothing else

The whole product is one loop. It is short on purpose: a session you can finish is a session you come back to.

01

Five ideas, picked for you

Tell it what you want to get better at and how long you have. The day’s five are chosen from 88 sources across 14 topics — arguments worth having, not headlines.

02

It ends

A session has a last page. Five ideas, about five minutes, then you are done and the app says so. There is no feed underneath it and nothing loads when you reach the bottom.

03

The ones you keep come back

Save an idea and it returns after a day, then three, then seven, then sixteen, then thirty-five. Two answers — you remember it, or you don’t. After the last one it stops asking, because you have it.

Anatomy of a card

This is one idea

Not a chapter, not a summary of a summary. A claim, the reasoning that makes it true, and something to do about it before tomorrow — with the source it came from named underneath. These are five real cards from the app.

The claim, in one line

The obstacle is information

Why it holds — the argument, kept intact

A blocked path tells you something about the terrain. Aurelius treats interference not as an interruption of the work but as the material the work is now made of. This is less inspirational than it sounds: it is a practical instruction to stop waiting for conditions to improve before you act.

Something to try before tomorrow

Ask of the current obstacle: what does this let me practise that a clear road would not?

The claim, in one line

The cost of a thing is the life you exchange for it

Why it holds — the argument, kept intact

Thoreau's most durable idea is an accounting one: price the purchase in hours of your life rather than currency. A more expensive flat is not more money, it is more years at a job you would otherwise leave.

Something to try before tomorrow

For the next thing you consider buying, divide the price by what you earn in an hour after tax. Decide whether you would trade that many hours of your life for it.

Where it came from, always named

BookWaldenHenry David Thoreau

The claim, in one line

You lose the ability to tell how impaired you are

Why it holds — the argument, kept intact

The consistent finding in restricted-sleep studies is not just that performance drops. It is that self-rated alertness stops dropping with it. People plateau at "a bit tired" while their measured performance keeps sliding. The instrument you would use to check on yourself is the instrument that breaks first.

Something to try before tomorrow

Do not use how you feel to decide whether you are fit for something that matters. Use how much you slept.

The claim, in one line

Pay yourself first, before anyone else

Why it holds — the argument, kept intact

A tenth of everything earned is kept before any obligation is met. The order is the whole idea: saving what remains after spending produces nothing, because spending expands to fill whatever it is given. Saving first makes the rest the variable.

Something to try before tomorrow

Move the transfer to the day you are paid, not the day before the next one.

Where it came from, always named

BookThe Richest Man in BabylonGeorge S. Clason

The claim, in one line

The dose-response curve is steepest at the very bottom

Why it holds — the argument, kept intact

The largest gains in mortality risk appear between doing almost nothing and doing a little. Going from sedentary to a short daily walk moves the number more than going from moderately active to very active. This is the least motivating finding in the field and one of the best supported.

Something to try before tomorrow

If you do nothing, the useful target is not a programme. It is ten minutes, most days.

Questions

Is this a book summary app?

No, and the distinction matters. A summary compresses a book so you can say you have read it. Mindroll takes one idea at a time, keeps the argument that makes it true, and then asks you about it again days later. You will finish fewer books here and remember considerably more of them.

Does this replace reading the book?

It is not meant to. Half of what it is good for is telling you which book is worth your next month — you read one idea from it before committing to four hundred pages. Every source is named, so going to the original is always one tap away.

What is actually in it?

88 sources — 39 of them books, the rest articles, podcasts, talks, essays and research — broken into 616 ideas across 14 topics. All of it is either out of copyright and summarised in our own words, or written for this app. Every item carries its licence and the date it was last checked.

How does the review actually work?

Only ideas you save enter review, because saving is you saying that one mattered. A saved idea comes back after one day, then three, then seven, then sixteen, then thirty-five. You answer one of two things: you remember it, or remind me. Forgetting sends it back to the start, and a review session is capped at seven so it always ends.

Will it work without a signal?

Reading works offline once the day has loaded. Downloading ideas and audio to keep permanently on the device is part of Pro.

What do you collect about me?

What the app needs to give you a personalised plan and remember your progress: your onboarding answers, what you read and save, and your subscription status. Usage analytics are off until you turn them on. We never see your card details, we do not sell anything about you, and the whole of it is set out in the privacy policy.

How do I cancel?

In your Apple account settings, in two taps, exactly like every other App Store subscription. We are not involved and cannot be a reason it fails. You keep Pro until the period you have paid for runs out.
